2026-08-19The codex is an object, and the first one was a theme. Four days of book-look work were shown to be the wrong thing: a page styled like parchment is not a book. A prototype of the codex as a physical object is published here for judgement — Gondorian boards and silver fittings, leaves that curve into a real spine, furniture mounted on the cover rather than floated on the page, and a superseded reading struck through by hand the way a scribe would cross it out. It is a specimen under review, not a hall: nothing in the archive depends on it, and it will be either adopted across the 621 routes or thrown away.
2026-07-27Everything answers to its name, and the silences say why. A day of asking the archive what it could not find. The three houses of the Edain — Bëor, Haleth, Hador — were drawn on the peoples tree and reachable by no search; so were the Broadbeams and the four Eastern houses of the Dwarves. The heraldry knew twelve devices of fifty-one. And 225 Elvish forms of the Etymologies could be found nowhere at all — searching for alqa, the Quenya swan, returned nothing, in an archive that holds the Etymologies entire. All of it is findable now, and read from the halls that draw it rather than typed beside them.

The books were allowed to overrule the map four times. Ossiriand remained, says the Silmarillion's own index, and the archive had drowned three places in it: Tol Galen, which the text puts beyond Gelion, remains; Sarn Athrad lay on Gelion itself and its fate is now recorded as unrecorded; Amon Ereb keeps its drowning and loses a parent it never had. The Argonath and Amon Hen were in Rohan, the Ice-bay of Forochel in Arthedain, the Dead Marshes in Gondor — four containments a drawn border asserted and no text supports, each dropped with the words that deny it.

The cirth were adjudicated against Appendix E, which says of the mode of Erebor that its peculiarities are “not included in the table”: the plate prints two rune-rows, and the page had announced three. The values carrying a question mark — the archive guessing in public — are gone. Side by Side offered “Aragorn vs Arwen” and answered with Aragorn I, dead of wolves in T.A. 2327, and with Eärwen, Galadriel's mother. And the Law of the Archive itself said the corpus was twenty-eight books; it is forty-nine.

Of 304 places, 121 sit within a realm and 183 now say why nothing contains them — thirty-eight are realms, thirty-six run through more than one, one is the world, and a hundred and eight are the archive's own silence, declared rather than left blank.
2026-07-27The forty-ninth volume. Vinyar Tengwar 43 has sat on the shelf unindexed since it was added, and the index builder has printed NOT INDEXED (add to DOCS/FILES) on every run it has ever made. Its feature is “Words of Joy: Five Catholic Prayers in Quenya” — Tolkien’s own Quenya: the Átaremma, the Aia María and the Alcar i Ataren, tier 2 by where they were printed and tier 1 for whose words they are. The shelf now reads 49 volumes, 6,885,537 words, and every page that quoted the old figure was corrected with it. A warning that fires on every run and changes nothing is a warning nobody reads.
2026-07-24The shelf grows. Seven documents added: the Revised and Expanded Letters (an excerpt, but carrying the full contents of all 508 letters and the text of thirteen never printed in 1981), Björkman's Emblems and Heraldry from Forodrim, and five issues of Parma Eldalamberon — which prove to be the 1971–74 fanzine, not the manuscript series, and are registered at tier 3 accordingly. The shelf is relaid by tier under sources/; 47 registered sources, 49 texts, 6.87M words.
2026-07-23The great polish. Sitewide overhaul: grouped navigation with plain-word subtitles and breadcrumbs, keyboard access and visible focus for every control, dark theme (☾ in the nav), readable citations, page descriptions and previews for sharing, crawlable pages for every realm, person and place, a sitemap and a proper 404, faster map loading, a fixed update-delivery bug (returning visitors now always get the newest archive), and these Annals.
2026-07-23The Realms of Arda. The political atlas: 60 polities from Valinor to the Reunited Kingdom with cited dossiers of government, law, economy, war, diplomacy, tongue and story; borders through thirteen slices of time; the Rise & Fall of every state on one strip.
2026-07-23The Reckonings of Arda. Every calendar and measure: Shire Reckoning to the yén, a date-converter across all reckonings and into our calendar (exact where Tolkien is exact), feast-days of every people, the ranga, the lár and the coinage of Gondor.
2026-07-22The Re-ingestion. All 28 volumes re-extracted (5.6M words), a corpus structure index, a 541-entity concordance ("In the books" panels on every record), an 18-item classified contradiction register, and a 521-date machine audit with conservative fixes.
2026-07-22The proposals completed. Offline app (PWA), Side by Side comparisons, and The Silences — the corpus's deepest gaps gathered and left honestly unfilled.
2026-07-22The Turning of the Ages & Spoken Arda. Map and chronicle joined on one slider through 21,000 years; every name speakable aloud by Appendix E's rules.
2026-07-22Place records & guided tours. A record page for every place, joining battles, borders, journeys and treasures; six narrated tours with the living halls beside the story.

2026-07-21Permanence. The archive deployed to its lasting home at samsikallo.github.io/arda-archive, with a self-contained offline copy.
