Of the silences

Where the corpus is silent, the archive says so exactly.

An archive that hides its gaps cannot be checked, and an unfalsifiable archive is a story. So the blank space on these leaves is ruled rather than filled, and every silence carries the name of whatever looked for the thing and did not find it.

A silence may only be asserted by something that looked. That rule was written after a fall-through branch in a generator published two hundred and eight claims that the corpus did not say what contained a place — a sentence about Tolkien reached by not matching three tests about the data.

It widened within the hour: a hand may not assert a silence it did not search either. A fall-through has a shape and can be swept for. A sentence typed by a careful reader has no shape at all, and looks exactly like a measured silence.

The cost of not looking is not only a false claim. It is a citation the archive owned and could not see. Of four false silences found in one week, one upgraded a claim: the placing of Taniquetil, recorded as an inference about where the Vanyar dwelt, is stated outright in the Silmarillion twice.

So a silence audit is not only a hunt for overclaiming. It recovers evidence.

The rule as it stands

Never write that the corpus does not say X without having asked ardatext for X — and without asking whether a neighbouring record already says it. [C]

Of the silences

An archive that hides its gaps cannot be checked, and an unfalsifiable archive is a story.a So the blank space on these leaves is ruled rather than filled, and every silence carries the name of whatever looked for the thing and did not find it.

The rule widened again within the day. A hand may not assert a silence it did not search, and a fall-through at least has a shape a sweep can find. A sentence typed by a careful reader has no shape at all, and passes every instrument the archive owns.f

What the sweep found

An abstract-syntax sweep of all 326 modules tested seven shapes of fall-through and qualified two. Between them they published 598 records asserting a silence nothing had measured — 382 portrait prompts, and 208 of the gazetteer's 481 places above.

The cost of not looking is not only a false claim. It is a citation the archive owned and could not see. [C]

What a silence costs when it is wrong

A declared silence is load-bearing. Later work builds inferences on top of it, and each inherits a weaker footing than the corpus actually offers. Of four false silences found in one week, one did not retract a claim — it upgraded one.

So the tally is kept where a reader can see it: 14 silence claims tested, four false, two written by a generator and two by a hand.

The rule as it stands

Never write that the corpus does not say X without having asked ardatext for X — and without asking whether a neighbouring record already says it. [C]

Fourteen silence claims have been tested and four were false — two written by a generator and two by a hand. Blast radius, not authorship: the fall-through publishes at scale, and the typed sentence is the one nobody can sweep for.f

Of the four, one upgraded a claim rather than retracting one, which is why a silence audit is run at all: it recovers evidence as often as it removes it.

The rule that widened twice

It began as a rule about one branch of one generator. Within the hour it covered every fall-through in the tree; within the day it covered a sentence typed by a careful reader, which has no shape at all and which no sweep can find.

The asymmetry is what makes it cheap to obey: asserting a silence costs one sentence, and falsifying one costs a single call to ardatext — a matcher that folds accents, case and line-breaks, which are the three ways a raw search reports a silence that is not there.g

A raw search for Théoden returns nought in The Lord of the Rings, where the name stands 261 times. [C]
