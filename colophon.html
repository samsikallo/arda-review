The colophon

Here the archive signs its own work: what made it, from which sources, when it was last built, what it holds — and, at the end, what it refuses to say.

A scribe closed a codex by naming himself, the house he wrote in, the day he finished, and by asking the reader to forgive the errors he knew were there.a This archive can say more than that, because everything about its making is recorded rather than remembered — so this leaf states the measurements instead of the apology.

What made it

No hand set this page. 221 of this project's published modules write a file the archive publishes, of which 110 are named as generators outright; the rest splice, merge, shard, classify or compose.b The count is not mine — it is what the archive's own migration gate finds when it parses every module and asks which of them writes into the published tree.

This leaf was last built on 2026-08-25 16:35 UTC. That single figure is the one thing here no later check can re-derive: the date of a making cannot be recovered from the thing made, so the guard behind this leaf verifies its shape and says plainly that it did not verify its value.c

The rule this leaf is built on — every number on these six leaves is generated from the record it describes, and none is typed. A later hand cannot even add one: the check refuses any digit in this book that is not carrying the name of the record it came from.

A colophon that overstates the archive would be the worst page in the building to get wrong. [C]

a The form is old and the honesty is not: a scribal colophon names the maker and asks pardon for the errors. This one counts them.

b map/gate_check.py — the archive's migration gate, which parses every module in map/ and answers which of them write a published file.

c map/colophon_check.py exempts the build stamp by name and states the exemption in its own verdict. An exemption nobody states is a silent one.

Volume VI · The Specimenviii

The sheet this book was measured against is not a hall of the archive and is not content. It is a specimen: five codex objects drawn on one page, approved by the owner, and never edited afterwards by anybody.d Every other leaf in this archive is scored against it, element by element, and where a leaf falls short the shortfall is a number rather than an impression.

The Specimen Sheet

It stays published, and it is presented as what it is: the specimen sheet, carried in the same volume as this leaf and named in the contents as a specimen rather than as a hall. A reader who finds it learns something true about how the archive was made, instead of meeting a hall that behaves unlike every other one.

In the binding it stands immediately before this leaf: turn back one and you are on it.

The grid reads 44 features off that sheet and scores 66 pages against them, structurally rather than by pattern — a feature nested in the wrong parent counts as absent, because a page once scored thirty-three of thirty-four while a single mistyped closing tag had swallowed its footnote band, its gathering signature and its catchword.e

What this leaf does not carry

The specimen sets a marginal hand and four hand slots on each of its leaves. This one carries none, and the reason is a record rather than an oversight: the archive holds 59 marginal hands, and every one is anchored to a line of the corpus it quotes. There is no line of Tolkien to anchor a note about this archive, so the margin stays bare.f

A thing that appears on some leaves and not others, for no reason a reader can check, is decoration. [C]

d C73, the owner's ruling of 20 August 2026. Edits to that page have been reverted twice; this leaf changes how it is described and never touches it.

e map/codex_coverage.py parses with the browser's own tree-construction algorithm, which is what makes it reproduce the swallow.

f C46: the marginal hand binds by entity, and where the archive holds none the margin stays empty.

A·viijFrom
The Arda Archive · The Sourcesix

From which sources

The archive holds 260 sources and grades every one of them, because a claim is worth what the book behind it is worth. 14,326,937 words stand in them, and 234 files are what the archive's own matcher will search when a citation is checked.g

The four shelves

The tier a book stands at is the register's own judgement, in its own words. Tier one is Tolkien himself, and Christopher Tolkien's editions, and 36 books stand there. Tier two is what the Estate approved: 29. Tier three is in harmony with Tolkien but not of his hand, and it is the largest shelf at 184. Tier four is outside the canon, used only where Tolkien would own the claim: 11.

There is no fifth shelf — and the register's fifth entry is not one. It reads made by this archive, and it is the FLOOR the archive's own work stands at when it is weighed against a book, not a place a source sits. Sources standing at tier five: 0. Two separate audits have read that entry as a contradiction in the register; it is not one, and this is the sentence that says so.

And the register names 12 books it does not hold. A source list that printed only what it had would tell a reader nothing about the shape of what is missing, which is the more useful half of the two.h

Where two sources disagree, the lower tier number wins. [C]

g map/ardatext.py, the archive's one matcher. The corpus is stored in mixed Unicode normalisation, and a raw search for a composed Théoden finds none of the occurrences that stand in The Lord of the Rings.

h site/arda_sources.json, its own not_held block. Each row names the book and why the archive does not have it.

Volume VI · What the archive holdsx

What stands on those shelves is 19,382 claim-bearing records, across 47 published datasets that have each declared where their claims live and where the citation for one is kept. 14,342 carry a citation or a confidence marker. 5,040 carry neither, and that number is printed here rather than kept in a log.i

A further 52 datasets are declared to make no claim about Arda at all — a list of which file holds which statement is machinery, and machinery carrying a confidence marker would be a marker about nothing.

At what tiers

Behind the records of persons, places and realms stands the apparatus: 5,676 attestations bound to 582 leaves, each carrying the tier of the volume it stands in — 3,550 in Tolkien's own books, 429 in Estate-approved ones, 1,667 in derived scholarship and 30 outside the canon.

Read that exactly — an attestation says a name stands in a line, which is a fact about the line. It does not say the line is about the record, and the archive does not resolve pronouns. A figure discussed for a page under he is attested once.
The six markers

Across 97 published datasets, 65,837 records carry one of the six: 15,197 corpus-attested, 1,458 high inference, 1,907 middling, 192 low, 47,048 from outside scholarship and 35 the archive's own coinage. A further 637 carry a value outside the six, frozen at that count by a guard and not growing.j

And the archive corrects itself in public. Its most recent correction reads “UT, Cirion and Eorl, note 36” in arda_quotes.json. The words it replaced are the verbatim string standing in the parent commit, and the guard re-opens that commit to prove it. It is the newest correction whose two readings both fit a margin and whose locus rests on the record's own identity rather than on its position in a list — which is the same test that refused the rows counted on the leaf after this one.

Every claim is either cited or confidence-labelled. [C]

i map/rule1_check.py. Three attempts to ask this question generically failed; the shapes are declared per dataset, and a dataset with no entry fails rather than being silently exempt.

j map/confvocab_check.py. A marker may be written bracketed in prose and bare in a field: the field is already the delimiter.

The Arda Archive · What it refuses to sayxi

What this archive refuses to say

The section a scribe could not write. These are not gaps waiting to be filled: each is a thing the archive measured, decided it could not honestly state, and counted.

It will not tell you why it changed its mind when it does not know. Its own history holds 11,336 real changes to published values; 1,410 of them carry a reason that can be attributed to a sentence naming the record, and those are published as errata. The other 9,905 — 87% of everything that ever changed — stand in the record with no stated reason, and no reason will be inferred for them.k

Why not mine them harder — recovering a reason from the context around a change is an inference about why somebody acted, and a wrong one produces an erratum that is verbatim and false: real words, real commit, invented motive. An apparatus that hid its own denominator would let a reader conclude the archive had changed 1,410 things when it has changed nearly eight times that.

It refused 220,299 further rows outright. They sit in 12,429 lists that have no stable identity key and that also changed length, so pairing row against row by position would marry unrelated records — and the erratum that came out would be verbatim, cited, checkable and wrong. Both errors here fail toward silence on purpose.

Withdrawn — the course of the River Ascar, crossing Gelion at Sarn That reading was retracted from arda_edge_scripts.json and the archive keeps the retraction where a reader can see it, rather than quietly deleting the sentence.
And the size it is built for

It will not tell you this book fits on a screen it has not been built for. The smallest viewport the codex is made for is 1600 x 1000 pixels, and the binding term is the HEIGHT rather than the width: the board is sized from the viewport less the header and the furniture, so a short window starves it however wide the screen is. Below that floor the archive makes no claim that a leaf holds its own record.n

Two smaller viewports — 1024 x 768 and 844 x 390 — are measured on every run and enforced on none, and their findings are printed rather than suppressed. That is 2 declared silences, not a clean bill: the leaves that clip there really do clip. What the archive will not do is quietly call them fine, and it will not print a total here either — the count moved by two across three runs of an unchanged archive, so it is a band and not an inventory. The command that produces today’s is beside it, which is how every number on a moving population should be written.

Ask it yourself — map/codexfit_check.py drives a real browser over every built leaf at every width above and prints the findings at each, marking the two below the floor with a star. A figure that moves between two runs of an unchanged archive is not a figure this leaf may set in type.

A silence is a measurement, never a fall-through. [C]

B·iijIt
Volume VI · The Colophonxii

It will not draw arms nobody granted. 218 houses, realms and places have a device corner on their leaf. The corpus lets the archive blazon 49 of them; the remaining 169 corners stay bare — 78% of them. That is not a backlog. Tolkien blazoned few houses, and the archive's own tier-one text says so outright; a device the lore cannot support is worse than a blank corner, because a blank corner tells the truth.l

Every bare corner was swept for first, and the searches are recorded against it. 6 subjects came back with nothing usable at all, which is a stronger statement than a blank and is filed as one.

Sound to codepoint stays refused

The archive can set Sarati as pictures and it can say which keystroke of one designer's layout draws which mark. It cannot say which codepoint holds a shape, because nobody has published the chart that binds them; the obvious guess would render confidently and be wrong. 3 published datasets refuse that question by name in their own text.

And the gloss that will not be built

The archive can gloss a name: 9,673 of them, each gloss carrying its source and its tier, and 247 such marks stand on 224 built leaves today. What it holds no record of is which phrase of its own prose earns a gloss — which is what the specimen's glosses are. Generating those would be inventing scholarship, so that feature may never be built at all.m

The signature — made by the Arda Archive out of the sources named on the leaf before this one, by the modules named on the leaf before that, and checked by a guard that reads this page rather than the data behind it. Whatever is wrong here is wrong in the record too, and can be found.

A claim carries its evidence, or it is not published. [C]

l C20 and C36 set a device in the corner of every leaf; the ceiling is quoted from the editorial note on Tolkien's heraldic devices in The Silmarillion.

m C44 and C54: the apparatus is editorial judgement, and generating it automatically is the one thing this archive is built against.
