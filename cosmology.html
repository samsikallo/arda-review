Volume VI

The Cosmology of Arda

The shape of the world from the corpus's own diagrams — the layered vessel of the Ambarkanta, the Change of the World and the Straight Road, the four ages of light, and the Door of Night — original art, every label Tolkien's own term
The shape of the world, from the corpus's own hand: the Ambarkanta — 'the Shape of the World' — with its diagrams stands in HoME IV; the Change of the World in the Akallabêth; the sequence of lights in the Quenta. The diagrams below are original renderings; every label on them is Tolkien's own term. Where the late writings unmake this picture, the contradiction is shown, not smoothed.

I · Ilu — the World-vessel of the Ambarkanta

'About all the World are the Ilurambar, or Walls of the World. They are as ice and glass and steel, being above all imagination of the Children of Earth cold, transparent, and hard. They cannot be seen, nor can they be passed, save by the Door of Night.' — the Ambarkanta
Source: The Shaping of Middle-earth, V 'The Ambarkanta' [C — with Tolkien's own diagrams I–III]; the terms Vaiya, Ilmen, Vista, Fanyamar, Aiwenórë, Ulmonan, Ilurambar are all the text's own. The rendering above is original art after Diagram I; proportions schematic as Tolkien's were.

II · The Change of the World

'But the world was diminished... and Valinor and Eressëa were taken from it into the realm of hidden things... the world was indeed made round.' — Akallabêth
Source: Akallabêth [C]; the Straight Road quotations Akallabêth ad fin. The white ships of the Havens leave by this road — see the Living Map's Grey Havens and the record of Círdan.

III · The Sequence of Lights

The archive's whole time-axis hangs on this sequence: year 0 = the first sunrise (see the timeline and the Reckonings hall). [C — Silm 1, 3, 8, 11]
The great unresolved: in the late 'Myths Transformed' (Morgoth's Ring) Tolkien tried a world round from the beginning, with the Sun elder than the Trees — a cosmology that would unmake the diagrams above. He never finished the change. The archive displays the published Silmarillion's picture and keeps the tension open — the contradiction register, 'Myths Transformed'.

IV · The Door of Night

'But Morgoth himself the Valar thrust through the Door of Night beyond the Walls of the World, into the Timeless Void; and a guard is set for ever on those walls, and Eärendil keeps watch upon the ramparts of the sky.' — Quenta Silmarillion, ad fin.
The one gate in the Ilurambar: made in the West (the Ambarkanta sets it in the Wall where Vaiya runs thin), it opens into Kúma, the Void. Through it the Enemy was cast; before it, by the older prophecy, he will one day return — a matter the corpus leaves at the edge of its own maps, which is where this page leaves it too.
Silm 24 [C]; Shaping, Ambarkanta [C]; the Second Prophecy's status: draft-layer only — see The Silences.
