The Arda Archive

A research-grade archive of Tolkien's legendarium, built corpus-first from 192 texts: living maps, timelines, family trees, languages, armies, realms, calendars — every fact cited, every silence declared.

Here the archive gives its name, as a codex does before it says anything else. What the name belongs to is a book: 647 openings bound in 7 volumes, and what a reader is holding is said across the gutter—

What you are holding

Every claim in it is cited to a source or carries the marker that says how far it is inferred, and where the corpus is silent the archive says so instead of filling the silence with something reasonable. At the back of the book the colophon signs the work and states what it refuses to say.

The fore-edge tabs above this opening are its 7 volumes and the way into each. This leaf belongs to none of them, as a title page belongs to no chapter: turn on from here and the book begins at Volume I — the contents.

The Law of the Archive

The books of the corpus are the only authority. Every statement carries its source; estimates carry their method and confidence — C canon I inferred (H/M/L) EXT external, fenced NEO not canon — and where the books are silent, the archive says exactly so: "The attached documents do not provide enough information to answer this." External maps, lexicons and scholarship were studied and credited, and never allowed to override a line of Tolkien's own. Contradictions the Professor left us — two Valian-year lengths, Gil-galad's fathers, Amrod's two deaths, the Angerthas value-shifts — are displayed, not resolved.
