IAniron cannot ship, and buying a licence would not fix it

The licence, read from inside the font itself

Not from a font-site's badge. This is name record 13 (License Description) in anirm___.ttf, the file inside aniron.zip as distributed by 1001fonts and dafont (both downloads byte-identical in content, 178 KB):

“The Aniron font is © Pete Klassen, 2004. All Rights Reserved. You are allowed to distribute this font free of charge only, and only with the complete contents of the original archive file included. You are allowed to use this font for private use only. Commercial use and/or modification of this font are not allowed.

Aniron is based on the credits font from “Lord of the Rings”. © 2001-2003 New Line Cinema and Wingnut Films. All Rights Reserved.”

Four independent blocks, any one of them fatal. (1) “Private use only” — a site on the public web is not private use. (2) “Only with the complete contents of the original archive file included” — a webfont serves a bare file; the archive would be distributing the font stripped of its own readme, which this sentence forbids even for a free, non-commercial site. (3) “Modification … not allowed” — subsetting is modification, and so is converting TTF to WOFF2. (4) The author states the design is New Line Cinema's and Wingnut Films'. Pete Klassen cannot licence what he does not own. A commercial licence bought from him would clear his claim and leave the studio's untouched — which is why no price is published and why no price would settle it.

Sources, four, independent: the font binary's own name[13] record (quoted above) · the readme.html in the same archive, which opens “© Pete Klassen, 2004. All Rights Reserved.” · 1001fonts.com/aniron-font.html — “Free for personal use / Not free for commercial use” (the page the owner linked) · dafont.com/aniron.font — “Free for personal use”, Pete Klassen, added 7 Dec 2004 · fontspace.com — “Freeware, Non-Commercial … Contact the designer to purchase a commercial license, if needed.”

Purchasability, measured: no commercial licence is offered anywhere. The author's site thehutt.de is alive; its Tolkien and fonts pages carry no licence, no price and no purchase route, and the site's own footer restricts its contents to “private, non-commercial” use. The only contact is thehutt@gmx.net, an address published in 2004. There is no price to quote because there is no offer.

And it could not set this archive even if it were free. Measured from the binary: 278 glyphs, zero combining diacritical marks, and no em-dash and no ellipsis — two characters the approved codex-proto.html already uses. It would break the archive's own page on day one.

The owner has already ruled, and his ruling set a condition that is now met

Recorded in fonts/Aniron-LICENCE.txt as landed in commit 33c1520, in his own words:

“for now the Arda Archive is personal use only, we haven't published it because it's not nearly done. I can get licence to publish it, until that use freely Aniron, if you don't find a better and higher quality similar one.”

So this page is not an argument against a ruling. It is the answer to the question the ruling asked. And the answer does not turn on the licence at all — it turns on whether Aniron can set this archive, which is a thing that can be measured rather than debated.

State of play, measured 19 Aug: commit 33c1520 is local only — origin/master is one commit behind it, and samsikallo.github.io/arda-archive/fonts/Aniron-Regular.woff2 returns 404 while the site root returns 200. Aniron is not on the public web. The decision can still be made before it becomes publication. The work already done is not wasted either way: the face sits behind a single --face-latin token in arda.css:23 and codex-object.css:22, so changing it costs two lines.

The measurement that decides it, with no reference to any licence

Aniron holds 278 glyphs, 270 codepoints. Counted across the 622 published HTML pages, 382,862 visible characters: Aniron cannot render 3,569 of them, 49 distinct, on 621 of the 622 pages — 100% of the archive. And the misses are not exotica. They are the punctuation of running prose:

The two columns are not the same kind of miss. Junicode's 1,318 misses are arrows, box-drawing and icons — furniture that falls back to a system font today and did so under EB Garamond too. Junicode misses zero characters of running prose. Aniron drops an em dash every few paragraphs, which means a visible change of face inside a sentence, on 621 pages. That is not a licence objection. It is the reason it is not the better face.

WWhat the owner actually pointed at, and this is worth separating from the font. Aniron's own author records two facts: the film credits use no capitals at all, and the face must be stretched vertically by 120% to match the DVD material. Looked at closely, Aniron's “lowercase” is small capitals — it is a unicase face. So a large part of what reads as “Aniron” is a setting, not a drawing, and a setting cannot be copyrighted. §4 shows the nearest openly-licensed drawings; the panel immediately below shows the setting, reproduced legitimately.

IIIThe corpus test — what actually breaks

The archive is mixed-normalisation and it is not a theoretical problem. Measured across the 15 corpus files that carry them: 2,636 decomposed base+mark sequences, 117 distinct, using 21 distinct combining marks. Eleven of those sequences have no precomposed form at all — ı̯ (×192), u̯ (×161), Ō̆ (×145), ā̆ (×113), ā́ (×113), ē̆ (×112), l̡ (×79), ı́ (×73), j̊ (×33) — so no normalisation pass can make them go away. Combining marks appear in 18 published HTML pages, index.html, languages.html, character.html and place.html among them.

The live defect this uncovered, independent of any typeface choice. The archive loads EB Garamond from the Google Fonts CDN. Google's latin and latin-ext slices carry only U+0304, U+0308 and U+0329; the vietnamese slice adds four more. Of the 21 combining marks the corpus uses, Google serves 7. 1,422 of 2,720 mark occurrences — 52.3% — are falling through to a system font on the live site right now. Self-hosting a subset that includes U+0300–U+036F fixes that whichever face is chosen.

Only the four self-hosted faces below can be tested honestly, because only they were subset with the full mark range. Théoden is shown composed (NFC) and decomposed (NFD) side by side; the last four columns are the corpus's real hard cases, two of which stack two marks on one letter and therefore need GPOS mkmk, not merely mark.

VThe cost, and the measurement that decides it

WOFF2 is Brotli-compressed already, so gzip over it gains nothing (measured: every file grew by 20–50 bytes). The wire figure is the WOFF2 byte count. Every subset holds the archive's full measured repertoire — ASCII, Latin-1, Latin Extended-A/B, IPA Extensions, all 21 combining marks, General Punctuation, the fi/fl ligatures. x@15px is the x-height the reader actually sees at the archive's reading size, measured from the glyph outline, not from a possibly-wrong OS/2 field.

What the owner is seeing when he says “Times New Roman”. Times has an x-height of 0.448 em. EB Garamond's is 0.409. He is not wrong; he is describing a real measurement.
