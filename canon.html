ask whether a phrase is Tolkien's — answered from the whole indexed shelf
Press Enter to ask. Accents are optional — Theoden and Théoden find the same lines.
What this can and cannot tell you. It searches the archive's corpus — every volume it holds, tiered, listed in the Corpus hall — for the words of your phrase, and reports every place where all of them occur on one line or on two neighbouring lines.

Two lines, because this corpus is hard-wrapped. 110 of its volumes break at about 72 characters, so a long phrase is usually split. Measured over 12,000 sampled phrases: a nine-word phrase falls on a single line only 42% of the time, and within two lines 88% — so a one-line search missed the majority of them. It missed Frodo's own greeting in The Lord of the Rings, where “a star shines on the hour” ends a line and “of our meeting” begins the next. Every answer says how many of its hits sit on one line and how many are carried across two.

A hit means the words are close together, not that they sit side by side. The index records where each word is, to the line; it does not record their order within it. So a hit is strong evidence and not a quotation, and this page will not pretend otherwise.

“Not attested” means this corpus does not hold it — not that Tolkien never wrote it. The shelf is large and it is not everything: some held volumes are image-only scans with no text to search, and they are named in the Corpus hall.

Long phrases are answered from their rarest words. A query of nine words is answered from the three least common, because the commonest carry hundreds of thousands of positions and would be fetched for nothing. The words actually used are named beside every answer.
